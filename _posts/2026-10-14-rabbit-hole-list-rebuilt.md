A list made the rounds this week: fifty legendary internet rabbit holes. You have seen its cousins before — the live counters, the statistics bureaus, the sites that are famous for being famous. I handed it to Muse and asked the only question that matters about a list like that: which of these would *I* actually open twice?

Eleven survived. Then Muse did the more interesting thing: it built the version of the list that fits this house. The original was all watching. Mine adds the making.

## The eleven keepers

- [Zoom Earth](https://zoom.earth) — live satellite weather, storms watched from the top down.
- [Windy](https://www.windy.com) — the weather map pilots and sailors actually use.
- [Lightning Maps](https://www.lightningmaps.org) — every lightning strike on Earth, near real time.
- [Submarine Cable Map](https://www.submarinecablemap.com) — the physical internet, drawn as the cables that carry it.
- [The Scale of the Universe 2](https://htwins.net/scale2/) — from quantum foam to the observable universe, one scrollbar.
- [Internet Archive](https://archive.org) — the library that refuses to forget.
- [Neal.fun](https://neal.fun) — small perfect interactive toys, including the deep-sea one.
- [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool) — data journalism as visual essays.
- [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org) — the long view, charted honestly.
- [Radiooooo](https://radiooooo.com) — pick a country and a decade, hear what was playing.
- [Hatnote — Listen to Wikipedia](https://listen.hatnote.com) — edits to Wikipedia, turned into ambient music.

What did not survive: the population counters and live-stat tickers (numbers going up is not a rabbit hole), and a national statistics bureau that turned up on the strength of somebody's copy-paste.

## For the bench

Parts for a breadboard computer are in the mail this week — a W65C02S processor, the modern descendant of the chip in my 1985 Apple //e. These four are the warm-up act.

- [Visual 6502](http://visual6502.org/JSSim/index.html) — a transistor-level simulation of the original 6502 die, built from microscope photographs of the chip: some 3,500 transistors lighting up node by node as code executes in your browser. This is the inside of the chip family on my bench. It is also, literally, a picture of my novel's epigraph — `LDA DARK` / `STA SPACE` / `NOP` — happening in silicon.
- [Easy6502](http://skilldrick.github.io/easy6502/) — a complete 6502 assembly tutorial with the assembler and simulator embedded in the page. Assemble, run, single-step, watch the registers change. The first lesson is `LDA #$01`. Good practice while the breadboard is in transit.
- [6502.org — Development Tools](http://www.6502.org/tools/asm/) — the long-standing index of assemblers and disassemblers for the 6502 family. The reference shelf, for when the code has to run on real hardware.
- [Internet Archive — Apple II Library](https://archive.org) — search "Apple II Library": thousands of //e-era titles playable in the browser, including the 4am Collection of copy-protected programs preserved in original form. My adolescence, bootable in a tab — and a preview of the software world the breadboard machine is being built to rejoin.

## For the grid watcher

- [Electricity Maps](https://app.electricitymaps.com) — live electricity flows and carbon intensity for more than 200 zones worldwide. My house runs on a dashboard philosophy — don't tell me, show me the flows — and this is that philosophy at planetary scale. Find California and watch what the grid around my Powerwall is actually doing.
- [PVWatts](https://pvwatts.nlr.gov) — the solar production model from the lab formerly known as NREL (renamed the National Lab of the Rockies, which is why the old nrel.gov address is dead), and the reason it earns a bookmark here: it is the model family behind the daily estimate my winter automations consult. Two of my Netzero rules arm themselves only when the modeled day falls under 10 kWh. Run your own array through it — mine is 4.51 kW, split as two runs for the 3-panel east array and the 8-panel west array — and you can see the typical day the gate is comparing against.

## For the musician

- [Radio Garden](https://radio.garden) — spin a globe, click a green dot, hear a live local station. Radiooooo travels in *time*; Radio Garden travels in *place*, live. Shortwave listening, rebuilt for the internet.

## The difference

A listicle knows what everybody clicks. A list knows where you live. Eleven of the fifty were worth keeping for anyone; the rest of this page only makes sense in a house with a Powerwall on the wall, a 6502 in the mail, and a radio habit. Build your own version — the exercise is the point.
