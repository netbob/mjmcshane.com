I posted my first video to a brand-new YouTube channel today — a weekly systems and outages report, narrated with an AI voice over simple ledger cards. The upload itself is straightforward. The gotchas are not. This is the walkthrough I wish I'd had, written from doing it for real on November 20, 2026.

## Before you upload: the channel

**Create the channel in a browser, not the app.** The YouTube iOS app has no create-channel option. Settings only lets you switch accounts. You need youtube.com in a browser (desktop works fine).

**Your first-choice name and handle may be taken.** I wanted "Field Report" and @FieldReport. Both were taken. "Field Report Tech" and @FieldReportTech worked. Have a backup in mind before you start.

**Know which channel you're on.** If you have a personal channel and a new Brand Account channel, they are separate. Everything below assumes you're uploading to the new one — so check before you start.

## The upload, step by step

### 1. Switch to the right channel first

Click your profile picture (top right) → **Switch account** → pick the new channel. This is the easiest mistake in the whole process: uploading while you're still on your personal channel puts the video on the wrong channel, and moving it later is a re-upload.

### 2. Upload the file

Click **+ Create → Upload videos** and pick your file. YouTube accepts MP4. While it processes, fill in the rest.

### 3. Title and description

Write the title as you'd want it to appear in search and on the channel page. The description should say what the video is about in the first two lines — that's what shows in search results — and include a link to any companion post or source page.

### 4. Thumbnail

YouTube offers three auto-generated stills from the video. Pick the cleanest one. You can also upload your own image — a title card works well. (If you upload a custom thumbnail and YouTube later asks you to verify your account by phone, that's normal; it's a one-time step.)

### 5. Audience: "not made for kids"

YouTube requires this setting. Select **"No, it's not made for kids."** "Made for kids" is for content directed at children — cartoons, nursery rhymes, kids' shows. Picking it for an adult video is wrong, and it disables comments and notifications on the video.

### 6. Altered content: disclose the AI voice

YouTube asks whether the video contains altered or synthetic content. If the narration is an AI voice, **say Yes**. This is especially true if the script has the voice saying "I'm [your name]" — a viewer could reasonably think that's your real voice. The disclosure adds a small label to the video. It does not hurt reach or ranking.

### 7. Playlists: skip for the first video

There's nothing to group a single video with. Once you have two or three videos in a series, create a playlist (for example, "Field Report — Weekly") and add each new video to it, so viewers can watch the series in order.

### 8. Video elements: subtitles, end screen, cards

**Subtitles — upload your own file.** YouTube auto-generates captions, but they garble proper nouns, incident numbers, and place names. If you have the exact narration script, make an .srt file from it (one caption per phrase, timed to the narration) and upload it: Video elements → Subtitles → Add → Upload a file. The result is exact words instead of auto-caption guesses.

**End screen and cards — skip them.** They exist to promote your other videos. On a channel with one video, there's nothing to promote yet.

### 9. Checks

YouTube runs a copyright scan. Wait for the green check. If it flags something, it will say what.

### 10. Visibility: Public, Unlisted, or Private

- **Public** — everyone can watch it. This is the goal.
- **Unlisted** — anyone with the link can watch it, but it doesn't appear in search or on your channel. Useful if you want to watch it on the channel first before anyone else sees it.
- **Private** — only you (and people you choose) can watch it.

**Premiere: leave it off on a new channel.** A Premiere plays the video live at a set time, with a countdown and a live chat. It's for building anticipation with an audience. With no subscribers yet, there's nobody waiting for the countdown — it just makes the first viewer sit through a timer before the video starts. Try Premieres later, once there's an audience to show up for one.

YouTube also asks "Do kids appear in this video?" before publishing. Answer honestly — for a narrated report over text cards, the answer is no.

Click **Save**. The video is live.

## Sharing it

YouTube's Share button will generate a post for X with the video title and a link. Two things to fix before you post it:

**Strip the tracking parameter.** The link YouTube generates carries a `?si=` parameter (for example, `https://youtu.be/fA3b0ky0TaQ?si=h_Lc9Afk0Z935Dfi`). It works, but it's clutter. The clean link is just `https://youtu.be/fA3b0ky0TaQ`.

**Write your own post text.** The auto-generated text is just the title plus a link. A short hook works better — something that matches the video's opening line. For my first video, I used the opening itself: the list of what went down that week, then the question the video answers, then the link.

## The checklist

- [ ] Channel created in a browser (not the app)
- [ ] Switched to the right channel before uploading
- [ ] Title, description, and tags filled in
- [ ] Thumbnail picked (auto or custom)
- [ ] Audience: "not made for kids"
- [ ] AI disclosure: Yes, if the narration is synthetic
- [ ] Subtitles: accurate .srt uploaded, not just auto-captions
- [ ] Visibility: Public
- [ ] Premiere: off (for a new channel)
- [ ] Share link cleaned of the `?si=` parameter

That's the whole process. The upload is the easy part. The gotchas are the channel switch, the audience setting, the AI disclosure, and the captions — and now you know about all four before you start.
