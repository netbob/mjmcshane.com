## Q1 — T5D04 — Ohm's Law (resistance)

**Question:** What is the resistance of a circuit in which a current of 3 amperes flows when connected to 90 volts?

- A. 3 ohms
- **B. 30 ohms ✓**
- C. 1/30 ohm
- D. 270 ohms

**Your answer:** D (270 ohms) — ✗

**Theory:** Ohm's law is **E = I × R**. To find resistance, rearrange it:

```
R = E / I
R = 90 V / 3 A
R = 30 ohms
```

The Ohm's law triangle — cover the value you want, read off the rest:

```
      E
     / \
    I × R
```

- Cover E → I × R (voltage = current × resistance)
- Cover I → E / R (current = voltage ÷ resistance)
- Cover R → E / I (resistance = voltage ÷ current)

**The trap:** 90 × 3 = 270. That's **E × I = power** (270 watts), not resistance. If the question asks for resistance, you divide, not multiply.

## Q2 — T5B09 — Decibels (power ratio)

**Question:** Which decibel value most closely represents a power increase from 5 watts to 10 watts?

- A. 2 dB
- **B. 3 dB ✓**
- C. 5 dB
- D. 10 dB

**Your answer:** D (10 dB) — ✗

**Theory:** Decibels measure a *ratio*, not an absolute value:

```
dB = 10 × log10(P2 / P1)
dB = 10 × log10(10 / 5)
dB = 10 × log10(2)
dB ≈ 3 dB
```

The two rules to memorize:

| Power change | dB change |
|---|---|
| × 2 (double) | +3 dB |
| × 10 | +10 dB |
| × 4 | +6 dB |
| ÷ 2 (half) | −3 dB |
| ÷ 10 | −10 dB |

**The trap:** 10 dB is the rule for **10× power** (5 W → 50 W), not for doubling. Going from 5 W to 10 W is a doubling, so it's +3 dB. If the question says "double," the answer is almost always 3 dB.

## Q3 — T3B06 — Wavelength formula

**Question:** What is the formula for converting frequency to approximate wavelength in meters?

- A. Wavelength in meters equals frequency in hertz multiplied by 300
- B. Wavelength in meters equals frequency in hertz divided by 300
- C. Wavelength in meters equals frequency in megahertz divided by 300
- **D. Wavelength in meters equals 300 divided by frequency in megahertz ✓**

**Your answer:** D — ✓

**Theory:** Wavelength = speed of light ÷ frequency. Light travels at about 300,000,000 meters per second, so when frequency is in megahertz:

```
λ (meters) = 300 / f (MHz)
```

Worked example — the 2-meter band:

```
λ = 300 / 146 MHz
λ ≈ 2.05 meters
```

That's why 146.52 MHz is the 2-meter band — the band your packet station will live on. The "300" is the speed of light in millions of meters per second, which is why the formula only works when frequency is in MHz.

## Q4 — T3C01 — UHF and the radio horizon

**Question:** Why are simplex UHF signals rarely heard beyond their radio horizon?

- A. They are too weak to go very far
- B. FCC regulations prohibit them from going more than 50 miles
- **C. UHF signals are usually not propagated by the ionosphere ✓**
- D. UHF signals are absorbed by the ionospheric D region

**Your answer:** C — ✓

**Theory:** The ionosphere reflects **HF** signals (3–30 MHz) back to Earth — that's skywave, and it's why HF goes long distance. **VHF and UHF** frequencies are too high to be reflected; they pass straight through the ionosphere and out into space. So a UHF signal only goes as far as line of sight — the radio horizon.

```
HF (3–30 MHz):   ionosphere REFLECTS it  →  skywave, long distance
VHF/UHF:          ionosphere lets it THROUGH  →  line of sight only
```

That's why packet radio on 2 meters is a local game (Fresno, ten miles across flat ground, is easy range) and why HF packet ignores mountains entirely.

## Q5 — T6C01 — Schematic

**Question:** What is an electrical diagram using standard component symbols called?

- A. Connection chart
- B. Instrumentation system
- **C. Schematic ✓**
- D. Flow chart

**Your answer:** C — ✓

**Theory:** A schematic is a circuit diagram drawn with **standard symbols** — a resistor is a zigzag, a capacitor is two parallel lines, a transistor has its own shape. The symbols are the same everywhere, so anyone can read the circuit regardless of language. The three figures in the pool (T-1, T-2, T-3) are schematics: T-1 is a transistor circuit, T-2 is a power supply, T-3 is an antenna tuner.
